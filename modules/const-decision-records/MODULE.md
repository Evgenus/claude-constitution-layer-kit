# MODULE.md — `const-decision-records`

*Draft at manifest version `0.0.2`, no open slots. This document is the module's description ([template](../../module-template/MODULE.md)). Every list and table row carries a handle.*

<a id="t1"></a>
## 1. Purpose

A session and its user argue a ruling through — options, objections, a pick — and the ruling stays in the chat. The next session finds the code changed and no reason why, and re-opens the question. Elsewhere a naming nit is written up with the same ritual as a structural choice, until nobody writes anything up. And a fix to the harness waits behind the very gate it fixes. The first failure compounds: every unrecorded ruling is reasoning that must be reconstructed later. And a document may call itself a decision record without saying what was at stake or who decided. Such a document is no better than the chat it came from. This module makes a decision record *valid at the moment it is written*. Its stakes and its decider are declared, or the write is blocked with a message that teaches the fix. The module also ships the two procedures around it: pricing and recording a decision, and promoting routine calls that keep recurring.

<a id="t2"></a>
## 2. Failure class

Split. **Accumulating:** records without stakes or decider, and rulings never recorded. Each one is a piece of reasoning a later session reconstructs by archaeology, and the cost grows with every such ruling. **Self-limiting:** mispriced ceremony — a team that ritualizes nits feels the drag and stops by itself; the triage guidance addresses it and no gate does. **Entry trigger:** day-one install set of every project that records decisions. The first real decision is the moment the gate and the creation skill must already be there.

## 5. Invariant

**Every Markdown document governed by a named record policy carries what a decision record adds to the envelope. These are `type: task-record`, `stakes` of `process`, `product` or `architecture`, and a non-empty `decider`. The document carries them at the moment it is written through the platform's file tools.** Policy, governed, envelope, stakes and decision record are the kernel's ([§1](../../docs/constitution/CONVENTIONS.md#k1), [§3](../../docs/constitution/CONVENTIONS.md#k3), [§4](../../docs/constitution/CONVENTIONS.md#k4), [§5](../../docs/constitution/CONVENTIONS.md#k5)). The word "named" is the dial `record_policies` of [§7](#t7). The phrase "written through the platform's file tools" is the honest boundary of [§9](#t9).

The advisory rules beside it, as a numbered list under the handles `R-a` to `R-f`:

1. **R-a.** Price a decision by its stakes before acting, routine calls leave a commit note and no record. *Advisory.*
2. **R-b.** A record is written from the skill's template, in one of the kernel's two body shapes, never with manufactured options. *Advisory.*
3. **R-c.** `process` takes the fast lane — the record and one human yes. *Advisory.*
4. **R-d.** A record is `active` while its ruling is being applied, `inactive` once applied, born `inactive` when applied in the creating commit. *Advisory.*
5. **R-e.** Until the yes or the review its stakes ask for, a record may still be corrected in place. The documents it changed are then corrected in the same commit, and the commit says so. Once accepted, reversing a ruling is a new record, and only a mechanical edit is made in place. *Advisory.*
6. **R-f.** Recurring commit notes on one theme are promoted to a record or a procedure. *Advisory.*

## 7. Dials

| dial | default | read by | meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `severity` | `block` | gate | `block`: a finding exits 2 and the write is blocked. `warn`: the finding is reported to Claude and to the user and the write proceeds — the adoption path onto a repository with legacy records. The scan ignores it. |
| `log_dir` | absent | gate | A repository-relative directory for the output log. The gate verifies with git that the directory is ignored and reports a configuration failure otherwise. Absent: the log goes to the platform's per-plugin data directory; where that is absent too (CI), nowhere, and stdout is the record. |
| `record_policies` | `[]` | gate | The ids of the policies that govern decision records. Empty: nothing is examined and every run says so. An id with no policy behind it claims nothing and earns a note. So does a policy whose namespace declares no territory, its note naming the line to write. |
| `boundary_examples` | `[]` | skill | The project's own routine / recorded pairs ([A3.5](../../docs/requirements.md#a3-5)): a list of `{routine: …, recorded: …}`. Absent or empty: the skill uses the seed pairs of [§8](#t8). |
| `deciders` | `{}` | skill | Who may decide at each stakes level ([A3.7](../../docs/requirements.md#a3-7)): `{process: […], product: […], architecture: […]}`. Absent or empty, as a whole or at one level: the skill has the agent ask who decides. |

The gate validates the three dials it reads at each read. A key of this namespace that is none of the six above is a configuration failure. A misspelt `record_policy:` would otherwise read as the dial unset. The three skill-read dials are refused by name only — a key that is not theirs — and read by the agent as written. ## 8. Boundary examples

**R-a** — **the routine / recorded line** (this module's slice of [kernel §4](../../docs/constitution/CONVENTIONS.md#k4)). The test behind every pair is one question — is the call cheap to revert? The pairs are the definition and are carried by the skill; the project's own arrive through `boundary_examples`. When two readings stay plausible the higher one is taken: an unneeded record costs minutes, an unrecorded structural call costs the next session its reasoning.

1. Where a worktree or a scratch directory lives → routine / which kinds of document exist and what authority each carries → recorded (`process`).
2. Where in a pull-request description a review note goes → routine / whether a change may land without a review → recorded (`process`).
3. How a test stub fakes a dependency → routine / how the product treats untrusted input → recorded (`product` or `architecture`).
4. Renaming a helper nobody else calls → routine / renaming an identifier, a setting or a contract that others already depend on → recorded.

**R-b** — **a real choice or not** (which body shape):

5. Three mechanisms were compared and one picked → options, pros and cons, one recommendation, the choice. / A schema gains the key a ruling already requires → ruling and reasoning, *not* two invented alternatives.
6. The decider overruled the recommendation → the record keeps both and says why / *not* a record rewritten so that the recommendation matches the choice.

7. One option was ever on the table, and a second is added "for balance" → *not* shape one; ruling and reasoning. / Two options were weighed in the dialog and one dismissed quickly → shape one, the dismissed option kept with the reason it fell.

**R-e** — **in place or a new record:**

8. A link form changes across every record, rulings untouched → in place, the commit names it a migration. / The substance of an accepted record's ruling changes → a new record naming the old one.
9. A typo in an accepted record — past the yes or the review its stakes ask for, or a `product` record once committed — → in place. The commit names it a typo fix. / An accepted record's `stakes` or `decider` "corrected" after the fact → *not* in place without saying so in the commit. If the ruling's price or owner was genuinely different, that is a new record.

10. An accepted record cites a document that has since moved → in place, a migration. / An accepted record's ruling no longer holds because a later ruling replaced it → *not* an edit and not a status flip. The later record names it, and the old one stays as written.

11. A validation before the yes finds a ruling item wrong → in place, the documents it changed corrected in the same commit. The commit says so. / The same finding after the yes → a new record naming the old one.

**R-f** — **promote or leave** (the promotion loop):

12. Three commit notes answer "where does this kind of file go", each a little differently → a recurring theme, and a missing ruling: record it. / Three notes mention the same directory while answering unrelated questions → they share a word, not a theme: leave them.
13. Two notes answer the same question in opposite ways → promote now, disagreement is the stronger signal. / Five notes give the same answer and every session finds it without asking → stable and known: leave it.
14. Everybody does the same thing, and each new session still has to rediscover it → a documented procedure, not a record of a choice nobody contests. / The answers vary with who was asked → a ruling is missing: a record.

The field checks are deterministic and need no examples; the fixtures of [§10](#t10) are their contrastive pairs.

## 9. Enforcement honesty

| # | artifact | kind |
|---|---|---|
| E1 | hook + `check-decision-record.sh` + `check-decision-record.py` (hook mode) | `gate`, severity dial `severity` (`block` default; `warn` reports and lets the write through) |

| E6 | the effect entry `effect-report.sh`: it informs judgment, gives no finding, never exits 2 and is never red | `advisory` |

**Honest limits.** The limits every gate shares stand in [kit-shared-gate-code §4](../../shared/GATE.md#g4) and apply here. This module's own:

1. **Off until named.** With `record_policies` empty the gate examines nothing. Backstop: every scan prints `0 documents examined` with the line to write. A hook run prints it too, to the debug log alone, since a plain line after exit 0 reaches no reader. Every session start prints the one line of [§6](#t6) while the dial stays so. A profile can prewrite the dial.
2. **A record outside record territory is invisible.** A document with `stakes` and `decider` in another policy's territory, or outside every territory, is not a record to this gate. Backstop: review; the friction signal of [§12](#t12), item 1.
3. **The rest of the envelope is not judged.** A record with a malformed `id` or `status` passes this gate. Backstop: module `const-meta-types`; alone, this module lets it through, and the install note says so.
4. **Whether a record was due is judgment.** A recorded-stakes ruling may be left as a commit note. A routine call may be recorded as `process`. A record may be made without the skill. No file shows any of them. Backstop: the skill's triage; review at the one yes.
5. **Body shape is not checked.** A heading check would pass manufactured options under an "Options" heading and fail a legal record that names its sections differently. Backstop: the template; review.
6. **Lifecycle is not checked.** Born-`inactive`, `active` while applied, and reversal by a new record are skill prose. An in-place edit of an accepted record's ruling is indistinguishable from a typo fix by its diff. And whether a record is accepted is not in the file. So a correction before the yes and a reversal after it look alike. Backstop: git history and review; a protected main branch where a PR flow exists.
7. **The skill-read dials are not validated.** A malformed `deciders` is read by the agent as it stands. The gate refuses a key that is none of the six, which is not validating the shape of what stands under a known one. Backstop: none — accepted, since the agent reads those dials as the project wrote them; the friction signal of [§12](#t12), item 4.

Exit codes of both modes: `0` green or nothing examined (the count line says which); `2` finding — the repository is wrong. Exit code `1` is a configuration failure — the checker could not run. Its causes are a missing or too-old interpreter, a missing library, a malformed dials file or a missing root policy. Another is the shared package missing or of a release that does not serve the module. Others are git absent, a log directory git does not ignore and unparseable hook input. The rest are a bad argument or an unexpected exception, whose traceback is printed. A record policy named in the dial with no policy behind it, or one whose namespace declares no territory, is none of these. It claims nothing, and its note says so. A key of the namespace that is no dial is one of these configuration failures.

**Depends on:** [kernel §1](../../docs/constitution/CONVENTIONS.md#k1) (the envelope and its lifecycle rule), [§3](../../docs/constitution/CONVENTIONS.md#k3) (territory, policy, kind, resolution), [§4](../../docs/constitution/CONVENTIONS.md#k4) (the stakes enum and ceremony). Beside the kernel it needs git, which the gate, the scan and the printer run to find the repository and to match territories. It needs Python 3.12 with PyYAML, which each entry file checks before anything else. And it needs the Python package `constitution-layer-kit`, which pip installs. That package is the kit's shared gate code and no sibling ([law 3 of CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md#law-3)). Nothing else.

**Kernel alone, with that package installed:** on a bare repository with no `constitution.yaml` every write passes with a visible zero count. There the session starts with the line naming the missing file, and both skills work from their seed pairs and the template. A dials file may name `module_prefix`, a root policy, a record policy and the dial. With such a file, every Markdown write into record territory is checked for the record fields, and the scan runs. The record policy is the consumer's document; the module ships none. Without git, the gate, the scan and the printer stop with the shared [`C3`](../../shared/MESSAGES.md#g3), exit 1, and say so. The two skills still load and work from their text, and the promotion skill's reading of the commit log needs git.

**Optional enhancements:**

1. When module `const-meta-types` is installed, the rest of the envelope and the resolution defects are guarded there. A record is fully valid per the kernel's record schema only with both.
2. When module `const-catalogs` is installed and lists a catalog whose territory holds the records, the skill takes the record's number from that module's allocation. This module reads nothing of that module's dial.
3. When module `const-questions` is installed, a recorded-stakes answer reached in its dialog is written through this module's skill.
4. When module `const-task-close` is installed, setting a record `inactive` at task close is its moment.
5. When module `const-locations` is installed, the skill takes a record's home from that module's command in its dry run. A kind and a stem go in, and a `HOME:` line comes out. The skill still writes the record from its own template. This module reads nothing of that module's dial.

## 12. Deferred

1. **Content detection** — treating any governed document with a `stakes` or `decider` key as a record. Signal: consumer commit notes about records found outside record territory.
2. **The envelope slice of immutability** — blocking a write that changes `stakes`, `decider`, `id` or `type` of a record that is `inactive` on disk, or flips it back to `active`. Deterministic, about fifteen lines, three rare false positives (a misspelt decider, a record set `inactive` too early, a record born `inactive` and corrected before its yes). Signal: a consumer commit note reporting an in-place reversal.
3. **Checking `decider` against the `deciders` dial.** The matrix gives [A3.7](../../docs/requirements.md#a3-7) to review. Signal: records landing with a decider the project's list does not allow.
4. **Validating the skill-read dials.** Signal: a session misled by a malformed `boundary_examples` or `deciders`.
5. **A marker that the skill ran in the session,** to refuse a free-form record. It proves "the skill ran earlier", not "this record came from it", and is a state machine over three hook events. Signal: free-form records recurring despite the skill.
6. **A record policy declared in the policy's own namespace** rather than in this module's dial. A kernel key, admissible when a second module needs to know which policies govern records.

7. **The effect report's other ways.** The report reads a record's territory by the record policies' own `territory` and not by a `policy:` declaration in an envelope. It counts a Write and not an Edit as the moment of W5. It gives the reader of W7 the six events around the human turn. So a record written later in a long session is outside its excerpt. A ruling made in free prose is no moment of W7. Signal: a session found, by review, to have skipped the skill or left a ruling unrecorded with the report not showing it, twice.

The deferrals every gate shares stand in [kit-shared-gate-code §1](../../shared/GATE.md#g1); this list holds the module's own.
