# Decision-record policy

Governs the document kind *decision record*: a record of one of the kit's own rulings, as [kernel §5](../constitution/CONVENTIONS.md#k5) defines it. Territory under this policy's id in `constitution.yaml`.

## Rules

1. <a id="r1"></a>**When.** A record is written for every `process`, `product`, or `architecture` ruling of the kit ([law 9 of CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md#law-9)); routine calls leave a commit note, never a record. Carrying an already-ruled item into a policy or into `CLAUDE.md`, its ruling unchanged, is a routine repair. The ruling's own record and yes cover it, and the commit note names that record. Unchanged is the limit. A repair that adds a condition, picks between two readings, or drops a clause is a ruling and is priced as one. A matrix row is not covered by this: amending one stays under rule 2 of [plc-0007-requirements](plc-0007-requirements.md#r2).
2. <a id="r2"></a>**Shape.** The kernel's record schema and body shape. A record applied in the commit that creates it is born `inactive`. A record's last section is titled "Carry list". Every ruling item opens with a bold handle, written `**R1.**`, `**R2.**` and so on. The explicit anchor of its own key, `<a id="r1"></a>**R1.**`, stands right before it. A citation of the ruling from another document links that anchor, `R7 of [dec-…](…#r7)`, with its record's id as the link's text. A citation that names several rulings at once links the first. Citations take this form save in a carry list, whose links lead to no record, and in a frozen research comparison. That comparison keeps the form it was frozen with. A deferred trigger, a fallback and an exception are items too, wherever in the record they stand. This holds though they change no file on the day. The list pairs each handle, once, with one of three things. First, links to the text that now states the item. An item that binds today is always carried so. Such a link may lead only where a rule can live. That is an `active` `current-state` document, `CLAUDE.md`, a skill of the kit, a module's or the template's own file, or a file that is not markdown. It leads to a file, never a folder. It never leads to the rollout plan, a research comparison, another record or a README, the root's or a module's, which binds nothing. A link that only names a module or qualifies a handle, as rules 6, 10 and 14 ask, is no home. In an entry that links its homes, the gate reads every link as a home, and review holds the difference. Second, "history only", for an item that binds nothing: a one-time act, a finding of the day. Third, "pending", for intent that is not yet real ([kernel §2](../constitution/CONVENTIONS.md#k2)). It comes with a link to the task-record that holds the work to come — a plan, never a decision record. The entry is never rewritten. When that task-record closes, its close carries the intent into the current-state documents. The gate says so in a note, and the next audit in full checks it. **The carry audit** is required before the yes. It is run through this repository's [`carry-audit` skill](../../.claude/skills/carry-audit/SKILL.md), by fresh readers of three kinds, and of a fourth where the change makes wording edits. One reads the record. It checks that the listed text states each item in full and hunts for binding sentences the list leaves out. It sorts every mention of the record elsewhere into provenance and reliance. One reads older records for regression — whether a file the change touched still states what they ruled. One never sees the record. Holding the current-state documents alone, it answers the questions the author puts, one per carried item. An item it cannot answer is a finding. The fourth reads each wording edit in accepted records or frozen comparisons against the kinds of rule 14 and [rule 7 of plc-0005-research](plc-0005-research.md#r7). Every finding is fixed, or justified in a commit message of the change, the merge commit included. It is never justified only in the pull request's description, which git does not keep. When fixes follow, a confirming reader is pinned to the commit that holds them and asked only what the fixes broke. This repeats while a pass finds a defect. The audit is run in full, over every record, on any change to the kernel and once before publication. **Enforcement, declared:** the shape of the list and the receipt of rule 5 are a gate, `scripts/check-carry-lists.py`, for records numbered `0030` and above. Whether a ruling is carried is judgment, held by the audit's readers and by review. The gate cannot see an item that was given no handle. Records `0027` to `0029` carry a list in an earlier form — another title, or items without handles — and records below `0027` carry none. An accepted record is not edited to fit a later rule (rule 14).
3. <a id="r3"></a>**Number.** Records are a numbered kind. Id and filename stem are one name, `dec-<nnnn>-<slug>`, and an entry of `const-catalogs.catalogs` in `constitution.yaml` sets prefix, width and territory. Its territory is this policy's own by a YAML alias. A new record takes one more than the highest number the catalog has held, deleted records included. The number is provisional until the record lands on main ([requirement A13.1](../requirements.md#a13-1), [A13.3](../requirements.md#a13-3)). Of two records that landed under one number, the one landed second is renumbered ([kernel §1](../constitution/CONVENTIONS.md#k1)).
4. <a id="r4"></a>**Reversal.** A ruling past its yes is reversed by a new record, never by editing the old one ([kernel §5](../constitution/CONVENTIONS.md#k5)).
5. <a id="r5"></a>**Yes.** The decider gives the yes by merging the change's pull request, or in chat, the session then merging in the decider's name. The merge commit names it: the commit that lands the change ([kernel §4](../constitution/CONVENTIONS.md#k4)) and completes it. For an audited record, that commit comes after the audit, since the readers are pinned to a commit that already holds the record. A decider who merges on the forge's page writes the yes and the trailers into the merge message there. A yes given in chat is complete with the merge that names it, and the window for corrections closes at that merge, whoever makes it. Until the merge of the kit's first pull request the kit had no PR flow. The yes was then named in the branch commit that landed the change, and those acceptances stand. The commit that names the yes carries two trailers in its last paragraph, as step 8 of [the carry-audit skill](../../.claude/skills/carry-audit/SKILL.md) words them. The first is `Accepted:` with the record's id — one trailer per record, or several ids separated by commas. The second is `Carry-Audit: at <commit>; <n> records; <f> findings, <x> fixed, <j> justified`, the receipt of the carry audit. It names the commit the last round of readers was pinned to, an ancestor at which the record already existed. It names the records read, above zero, and the findings of every round, each fixed or justified. A change is complete when it is accepted with a receipt the gate reads as right. The newest acceptance of a record counts. So a wrong receipt is mended by accepting again in the merge commit of a new pull request, never by rewriting history. Until its yes, the findings of a record's validation and of its carry audit may correct it in place, the commit saying so ([kernel §5](../constitution/CONVENTIONS.md#k5)). Records numbered below `0030` had their yes before these trailers existed — named in their own text or in a commit message — and are accepted. A record that no commit accepts yet awaits its yes. The gate reports a note and never a finding, since a gate is not red for waiting on a person. An `Accepted:` id that names no record is a note too — deleted since, renumbered, renamed or misspelt. Once a renumbered or renamed record is accepted again under its new id, its old id's note stops. It stops where git's rename detection follows the old id's file to a record that stands. A commit that holds the last rename must accept that record. A deleted or misspelt id keeps its note. So does a rename that git does not detect, one never accepted again, and one accepted only before the rename. A renumbered record is accepted again under its new id, in the merge commit of the change that renumbers it. That receipt is the one for the carry audit of the renumbering change. Its readers read the renumbered record in full. The audit's scope lists it under its new name, as a record the change adds. A record may be renamed as [kernel §1](../constitution/CONVENTIONS.md#k1) allows, when its catalog's form changes. It is then accepted again under its new id, in the merge commit of the change that renames it. That receipt is the one for the carry audit of the renaming change. Its readers read the renamed record in full. The audit's scope lists it under its new name, as a record the change adds. A renumbering or rename that merged without the acceptance under the new id is mended as a wrong receipt is. The merge commit of a new pull request accepts the record under its new id. Its receipt is for a carry audit that reads the record in full. In all three cases the commit the receipt names is one at which the record already stood under its new id. A record numbered below `0030` stays accepted with no trailer after its rename. The receipt shows that an audit was declared and accounted for, not that it was run.
6. <a id="r6"></a>**Enforcement.** Module [`const-decision-records`](../requirements.md#const-decision-records) guards `type`, `stakes` and `decider` of every document in this territory, at write time when a session loads the plugin, and by its scan. Its dial `record_policies` in `constitution.yaml` names this policy's id. The rest of the envelope is module [`const-meta-types`](../requirements.md#const-meta-types)'. Module [`const-catalogs`](../requirements.md#const-catalogs) guards the name, the id and a new item's number, free in the working tree, the same two ways. It also guards every collision between items that exist in the tree it scans, in its scan alone. Module [`const-referencing`](../requirements.md#const-referencing) guards links, anchors and, in its scan alone, the `dec-` mentions outside Markdown. `scripts/check-carry-lists.py` guards the carry list's shape, the receipt and the anchor before every bold ruling handle outside fenced blocks, in every record whatever its number. A record whose items carry no bold handle has none to hold. The kit's `scripts/check-handle-forms.py` holds that a citation of a ruling from another document links that anchor. It holds this in every Markdown file git lists save a research comparison, a section titled "Carry list", code blocks, code spans and HTML comments. A frozen comparison keeps the form it was frozen with. In one not yet frozen, and for a citation that names its record indirectly, "that record's R3", review holds the anchor. From record 39 on, `scripts/check-carry-lists.py` holds the order and contiguity of the bold ruling handles of rule 10, outside fenced blocks and before the carry list. From the same record on, `scripts/check-handle-forms.py` holds a rule for table rows outside the carry list. No such row has a first cell whose first handle is of the form `R<n>` and unqualified. The order and the contiguity of other handles, and of the handles of an earlier record, are review. The uniqueness of the bold ruling handles, from record 30 on, `scripts/check-carry-lists.py` holds. The uniqueness of other handles is review. Both scripts are entries of the gate list. The body's shape and the yes itself are review too. The twelve traces of the style defaults are held by module [`const-styling`](../requirements.md#const-styling) in its scan alone, which runs no hook. Module [`const-language`](../requirements.md#const-language) holds the grade, the sentence length and the constructions of the prose. Module [`const-doc-budgets`](../requirements.md#const-doc-budgets) holds the length in characters, against the ceiling its dial `ceilings` in `constitution.yaml` sets for documents of this kind. Both hold them after a write and in their scans. A threshold, a list or a ceiling an accepted record fails is met by the wording edits of rule 14 where they reach it. What no such edit meets is declared as a departure of that gate's dial in `constitution.yaml`, with its reason. A trace of a style default is declared the same way. The departure is set over the record's path or a number range that holds it. The list-handles scan of module [`const-interaction`](../requirements.md#const-interaction) and the marker scan of module [`const-questions`](../requirements.md#const-questions) read documents of this kind too and have no such dial. A plain list item in an accepted record takes a bold lead, and a marker it quotes takes a code span. The kit's `scripts/check-module-links.py` reads them too, in the gate list alone. A plain "module `const-x`" in an accepted record becomes a link to the module's specification, by a reference-form migration. An accepted record is never taken out of any gate's territory.
7. <a id="r7"></a>**Deletion.** No record has to be deleted. An `inactive` record whose ruling is carried binds nothing, and no rule depends on it ([kernel §5](../constitution/CONVENTIONS.md#k5)). It is kept as evidence of the options and reasoning. Whether anything should still reference it ([kernel §1](../constitution/CONVENTIONS.md#k1)) is the decider's judgment, not a count of links. Deleting one is routine and is one commit. That commit removes every link to it and the record itself. Its message names the record and calls the change a deletion. Inside another record, in a frozen comparison and in a done line of the plan, the link is replaced. It becomes the deleted record's title in plain words, never its id. So no link points at what is gone. Inside a record that is a reference-form migration in the kernel's sense, the ruling untouched. In a comparison it is an edit the freeze of [rule 7 of plc-0005-research](plc-0005-research.md#r7) allows by name. In the plan it is a routine edit. The commit names the deletion and, where a record was edited, the migration. A record or a frozen comparison sometimes quotes the deleted record's id or path. There, a quote in a code span, a fenced or indented block, or an HTML comment stays as written, as quoted evidence. No edit reaches it. Any other mention of the id or the path, in a record or not, the deletion commit removes. That includes a comment in a script or a configuration file and an entry of the dials file. Outside Markdown the `mentions` scan of module [`const-referencing`](../requirements.md#const-referencing) asks for it, and in Markdown review does. An `active` record is never deleted.
8. <a id="r8"></a>**Template.** A record is written from the kit's own template, `templates/decision-record.md`, which `const-locations.templates` names for this kind. It is the shape the record skill of module [`const-decision-records`](../requirements.md#const-decision-records) ships to every consumer, with what rules 2, 9, 11 and 12 add. Those are the carry list, the handles, the Applied section, the wording of the status and of the Choice. They are the kit's rules and no module's. The template carries every section and wording those rules require; a change to any of them changes it in the same change.
9. <a id="r9"></a>**Sections.** Shape one, when a real choice existed: Context, Options, Recommendation, Choice, Ruling, Applied where the record says what its creating commit did, Carry list. Shape two: Context, Ruling, Reasoning, Carry list. A record that answers several questions has one numbered section per question. Each has its context, its lettered options, one recommendation with its reasoning — one sentence is enough — the choice, and the ruling opening with its handle. An objection the session keeps against the choice stands in the record. The title is one line that states the ruling, never the topic, and the top-level heading repeats it.
10. <a id="r10"></a>**Items and handles.** A ruling item is any sentence that binds after the yes, wherever in the record it stands. A default the session took and left open to override is one too. The test is whether the blind reader of rule 2 would need it to answer. From record 39 on, the record that brought this sentence, handles run in order, contiguous and unique in the record, tables included. An earlier record keeps the handles it was accepted with, though from record 30 on no ruling handle opens two ruling items, which `scripts/check-carry-lists.py` holds. In every record, a key of another document is written qualified — "message R4 of the module". One kind of home per handle — links, "history only" or "pending". An item partly binding and partly history is split in two, or carried with its history written after the link. Handles sharing one home may be grouped in one entry. The entry form is the gate's, `R1 — …`.
11. <a id="r11"></a>**Status.** A record is `inactive` once every item has its home in the sense of rule 2, and `active` only while an item has none. An item has its home when stated in a current-state document, marked "history only" because it binds nothing, or "pending" in the plan. The plan is the write-head that holds intent not yet real until it is ([kernel §2](../constitution/CONVENTIONS.md#k2)). The kit reads the kernel's "applied" so: a pending item's ruling is that the work is planned. A record may grow while its change is made — a module's rulings record, a record whose slots are decided one per message. Such a record is not applied in its creating commit: it is born `active` and is `inactive` by the last commit of its branch. The merge that names its yes, or that ends its review, lands that commit — a pull request's merge edits no file. A record applied in its creating commit is born `inactive`, as the kernel says. A `product` record, having no window, cannot grow and follows the first four sentences alone.
12. <a id="r12"></a>**Body facts.** The body says "the decider". The Choice names where and when the pick was made — "in chat on <date>". It does so since a yes given in chat reaches git only with the commit that names it. A commit hash may stand in the body as [requirement A5.6](../requirements.md#a5-6) allows, never in a sentence that states how things are. An audit's pin, where git holds a deleted file that is no record, and a dated fact are such uses. A deleted record is named by its title, as rule 7 says.
13. <a id="r13"></a>**Reversal mechanics.** A reversing record names the reversed item by record and handle, and the reversal itself is carried "history only". The reversed record is not touched. A pointer to the reversing record added to an accepted record is new content, not the mechanical edit of [kernel §5](../constitution/CONVENTIONS.md#k5). The kernel's precedence clock already orders the two.
14. <a id="r14"></a>**Earlier records.** An accepted record is never edited to fit a later rule, whatever the rule. Only a typo fix, a wording edit of a kind this rule lists, and the reference-form migrations this policy names for that are allowed. These migrations are the module link of rule 6 and this rule's qualified handle, plain words, ruling's anchor and moved place. A migration that follows a document moved, renumbered or deleted, or a handle renamed, is the kernel's, at any time. The moved place: a change may move the whole text of a place into another document. A record's link to that place then takes, in the same change, the document and the anchor the text moved to. Only the link's target changes, and the link's text where it is the id of the document the text left. That text takes the id of the document it moved to. The record's other words keep the old name as history. The commit names it a migration and names the move (R9 of [dec-0115-long-documents-split-and-carry-entries-follow-moved-text](../decisions/dec-0115-long-documents-split-and-carry-entries-follow-moved-text.md#r9)). A later change may instead have rewritten the cited text into another document. Where a later record's carry list names that place for the same ruling, the link may take it at any later time. Only the link's target changes. The commit then names it a migration and names that record. A link to several places, or one place whose text moved only in part, keeps its target while any text it cites still stands there. Else it moves to where the first text it cites now stands (R11 of [dec-0115-long-documents-split-and-carry-entries-follow-moved-text](../decisions/dec-0115-long-documents-split-and-carry-entries-follow-moved-text.md#r11)). A heading, or a line that only links where the text moved, is no text of a place and no text a link cites. The exception is where that heading or line itself states a ruling the link cites. In a carry list, the text a link cites is the text that states a ruling of its entry. R11 of [dec-0115-long-documents-split-and-carry-entries-follow-moved-text](../decisions/dec-0115-long-documents-split-and-carry-entries-follow-moved-text.md#r11) says so. The documents a record cites, in the kernel's words for that migration, include every file and folder the record links to, document or not. After a file or a folder moves or is renamed, a link to it takes the new path, and only the link's target changes. The link's text, a code span and the record's prose keep the old name or path as written, as history. A document whose id changes, by a renumbering or a rename that [kernel §1](../constitution/CONVENTIONS.md#k1) allows, is outside the two sentences before this one. What its references take is said by that section and by the rest of this rule. Such a renumbering or rename changes a record's `id` and its file name only, and is no edit of its body. Rewriting a link to a renumbered or renamed record inside another record is a reference-form migration in the kernel's sense. It leaves the ruling untouched and is applied in place. The commit names it a migration and names the renumbering or the rename. So is rewriting, inside another record, each bare id that means a renumbered or renamed item of any catalog. Such an id stands outside a link, in prose or in a code span. It is rewritten to the item's new id in the form it stood in: the full id, or the prefix and the number alone. It follows [kernel §1](../constitution/CONVENTIONS.md#k1), which rewrites the references to the item. The commit names it a migration and names the renumbering or the rename. An id quoted as evidence stays as written: a form shown, the data of a measurement, the words of a quotation. So does any id in a fenced or indented block or an HTML comment. So does an id that means another item, such as the one that kept the number. A handle migration is a reference-form migration too, the ruling untouched. Where a record cites a handle of another document, its carry list included, the citation of the handle changes in three cases and no other. A migration of the citation's link stands as its own rule says. To a renumbered or renamed record, it is as above; to a deleted record's title, as rule 7 says, the handle kept. To the anchor of a ruling, it is as this rule allows, in the form rule 2 gives. (i) That document renamed the handle: the citation takes the new one, and a link's fragment that holds the handle follows it. (ii) The handle stands bare, and a reader could take it for a key of the record or of the kit. It is then qualified by its document's name or by a link to that document. In a carry list, a link qualifies it only where the link leads to no record, as rule 2 says. (iii) Its document lies outside the repository and was never in it, or holds no item under that handle. The handle then becomes the plain words it stood for. In each case the new words stand where the grammar of the sentence needs them, and nothing else in the sentence changes. The commit names it a migration, and names the rename in case (i) and the case in (ii) and (iii). Where a rule of this policy applies from a number on, the rule names the threshold, and records below it are not brought to it. A wording edit ([kernel §5](../constitution/CONVENTIONS.md#k5)) leaves the record's meaning as it stands. It changes no ruling, fact or number, removes or alters no link, anchor, code span or existing handle, and changes no word of a quotation. It is one of these kinds and no other. (a) A sentence split between two independent clauses. It is never made at a colon or at a dash that closes a pair of dashes. Nor is it made anywhere after a colon that opens a list or an explanation in the same sentence. Such a list keeps its lead, and kind (e) serves it. The dash, semicolon or comma there becomes a full stop. The next word takes a capital unless it is code or the text of a link. A handle whose first word is a word, such as "rule 4 of …", takes the capital as any word does. A joining word there — *and*, *but*, *so*, *while* — is dropped. A clause whose subject was shared takes it again, as the same noun or a pronoun for it. (b) A list item that opens with no list handle has its opening words set in bold. At most its words before the first colon, semicolon or full stop that ends a phrase are set so, that mark included. A comma may stand inside them. No word is added, dropped or moved. (c) A word that the `simpler` list of module [`const-language`](../requirements.md#const-language) pairs with a plain word is replaced by that plain word. An article before it is made to agree. (d) This kind serves a quotation, or a word mentioned as a word, that holds a phrase of that module's `forbidden` or `simpler` list. It also serves one that holds a marker of module [`const-questions`](../requirements.md#const-questions). Such a quotation or word is put whole in a code span, its words unchanged and its quotation marks outside the span. (e) A list written inside a sentence is set as a bulleted list under its lead. Such a list is items that follow a colon, or the bold lead of a list item, separated by semicolons. Each item becomes one bullet, its words and its closing semicolon or full stop unchanged. The bullets are nested under the list item the list stood in, if it stood in one. The text after the last item becomes a paragraph of its own. A bullet that opens with no list handle takes kind (b). (f) The anchor of rule 2, `<a id="r<n>"></a>`, is set right before an existing bold ruling handle of the same number. No other character changes. A wording edit is routine. The kernel allows it at any time. The kit makes it only in a change that has a carry audit, whose readers read each such edit against these kinds. The commit names it a wording edit and names the records it edits. A typo fix, which [kernel §5](../constitution/CONVENTIONS.md#k5) also allows at any time, corrects a misspelt word, a doubled word or a wrong punctuation mark. It also corrects a gendered pronoun that stands for a person. He, him, his, himself, she, her, hers or herself becomes the form of they that the grammar asks, they, them, their, theirs or themselves. The verb of its clause is made to agree. A typo fix changes no ruling, fact, number, link, anchor, code span, handle or word of a quotation. The commit names it a typo fix and names the records it edits.
15. <a id="r15"></a>**Other stakes.** Every record of the kit so far is `process`. An `architecture` record's review, which [kernel §5](../constitution/CONVENTIONS.md#k5) leaves the project to define, is the two passes of step 6 of [the module-development page](../guide/developing-a-module.md#s6). Every finding is fixed or justified. The first pass is a fresh reader holding the record, the current-state documents it changes and this policy, never the authoring reasoning. The second is the carry audit. The review is complete with the merge that accepts the record, once the decider has said so. The decider says so in chat or by that merge, where rule 5 puts the yes. That merge commit carries the two trailers of rule 5 as the receipt. So the correction window of rule 5 closes where [kernel §5](../constitution/CONVENTIONS.md#k5) closes it for this stakes, at the review's end. A reviewer other than the decider replaces that first pass once the kit has a second human. This takes an amendment of this rule under [rule 3 of plc-0001-root-policy](plc-0001-root-policy.md#r3). A `product` record, which the kernel gives neither a yes nor a review, is complete when committed and has no window. The carry audit is run on the commit that holds it. A finding is fixed in the documents or by a further record, never by editing the record beyond the mechanical edits of [kernel §5](../constitution/CONVENTIONS.md#k5). The merge commit of the change that holds the fixes carries the `Carry-Audit:` trailer alone. The gate's note that the record awaits a yes is the honest limit of a gate that does not read stakes.
16. <a id="r16"></a>**Style.** Documents of this kind follow the style guides that the dial `guides` of module [`const-styling`](../requirements.md#const-styling) names in `constitution.yaml`, the dials file. They depart from a default only where that module's dial `departures` names one for their territory, with its reason ([rule 9 of plc-0001-root-policy](plc-0001-root-policy.md#r9)). The traces of the defaults are held as the enforcement rule says, and the rest is review. A trace of a default in an accepted record is removed by a wording edit of rule 14 where one removes it. Where none does, it is declared by a departure over the record's path or a number range that holds it. It is never declared by taking accepted records out of the scan's territory. This follows decisions [dec-0045-frozen-documents-stay-in-the-style-scan](../decisions/dec-0045-frozen-documents-stay-in-the-style-scan.md) and [dec-0048-frozen-documents-take-wording-edits](../decisions/dec-0048-frozen-documents-take-wording-edits.md).
