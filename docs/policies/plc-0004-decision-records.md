# Decision-record policy

Governs the document kind *decision record*: a record of one of the kit's own rulings, as [kernel §5](../../kernel/CONVENTIONS.md#k5) defines it. Territory under this policy's id in `constitution.yaml`.

## Rules

1. <a id="r1"></a>**When.** A record is written for every `process`, `product`, or `architecture` ruling of the kit ([law 9 of CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md#law-9)); routine calls leave a commit note, never a record. Carrying an already-ruled item into a policy or into `CLAUDE.md`, its ruling unchanged, is a repair and routine — the ruling's own record and yes cover it, and the commit note names that record. Unchanged is the limit: a repair that adds a condition, picks between two readings, or drops a clause is a ruling and is priced as one. A matrix row is not covered by this: amending one stays under rule 2 of [plc-0007-requirements](plc-0007-requirements.md#r2).
2. <a id="r2"></a>**Shape.** The kernel's record schema and body shape; a record applied in the commit that creates it is born `inactive`. A record's last section is titled "Carry list". Every ruling item opens with a bold handle, written `**R1.**`, `**R2.**` and so on; a deferred trigger, a fallback and an exception are items too, wherever in the record they stand and though they change no file on the day. The list pairs each handle, once, with one of three things. First, links to the text that now states the item: an item that binds today is always carried so, and such a link may lead only where a rule can live — an `active` `current-state` document, `CLAUDE.md`, a skill of the kit, a module's or the template's own file, a file that is not markdown; a file, never a folder — and never to the rollout plan, a research comparison, another record or the README. Second, "history only", for an item that binds nothing: a one-time act, a finding of the day. Third, "pending" with a link to the task-record that holds the work to come — a plan, never a decision record — for intent that is not yet real ([kernel §2](../../kernel/CONVENTIONS.md#k2)); the entry is never rewritten: when that task-record closes, its close carries the intent into the current-state documents, the gate says so in a note, and the next audit in full checks it. **The carry audit** is mandatory before the yes and is run through this repository's `carry-audit` skill, by fresh readers of three kinds, and of a fourth where the change makes wording edits. One reads the record: checks that the listed text states each item in full, hunts for binding sentences the list leaves out, and sorts every mention of the record elsewhere into provenance and reliance. One reads older records for regression — whether a file the change touched still states what they ruled. One never sees the record: holding the current-state documents alone, that reader answers the questions the author puts, one per carried item, and an item it cannot answer is a finding. Where the change makes wording edits in accepted records or frozen comparisons, one more reader reads each against the kinds of rule 14 and of [rule 7 of plc-0005-research](plc-0005-research.md#r7). Every finding is fixed, or justified in a commit message of the change — in the pull request once the kit has one. When fixes follow, a confirming reader is pinned to the commit that holds them and asked only what the fixes broke, and this is repeated while a pass finds a defect. The audit is run in full, over every record, on any change to the kernel and once before publication. **Enforcement, declared:** the shape of the list and the receipt of rule 5 are a gate, `scripts/check-carry-lists.py`, for records numbered `0030` and above; whether a ruling is carried is judgment, held by the audit's readers and by review, and the gate cannot see an item that was given no handle. Records `0027` to `0029` carry a list in an earlier form — another title, or items without handles — and an accepted record is not edited to fit a later rule (rule 14); records below `0027` carry none.
3. <a id="r3"></a>**Number.** Records are a numbered kind: id and filename stem are one name, `dec-<nnnn>-<slug>`; prefix, width and territory are an entry of `const-catalogs.catalogs` in `constitution.yaml`, its territory this policy's own by a YAML alias. A new record takes one more than the highest number the catalog has held, deleted records included, provisional until it lands on main ([requirement A13.1](../requirements.md#a13-1), [A13.3](../requirements.md#a13-3)).
4. <a id="r4"></a>**Reversal.** A ruling past its yes is reversed by a new record, never by editing the old one ([kernel §5](../../kernel/CONVENTIONS.md#k5)).
5. <a id="r5"></a>**Yes.** Until the kit publishes (rollout Phase D) the yes is given in chat and named in the message of the commit that lands the change ([kernel §4](../../kernel/CONVENTIONS.md#k4)) — the commit that completes it, which for an audited record comes after the audit, since the readers are pinned to a commit that already holds the record; from then on it is PR approval on a protected main. The commit that names the yes carries two trailers in its last paragraph: `Accepted:` with the record's id — one trailer per record, or several ids separated by commas — and `Carry-Audit: at <commit>; <n> records; <f> findings, <x> fixed, <j> justified`, the receipt of the carry audit: the commit the last round of readers was pinned to, an ancestor at which the record already existed; the records read, above zero; the findings of every round, each fixed or justified. A change is complete when it is accepted with a receipt the gate reads as right: the newest acceptance of a record is the one that counts, so a wrong receipt is mended by accepting again in a new commit, never by rewriting history. Until its yes the findings of a record's validation and of its carry audit may correct it in place, the commit saying so ([kernel §5](../../kernel/CONVENTIONS.md#k5)). Records numbered below `0030` had their yes before these trailers existed — named in their own text or in the prose of a commit message — and are accepted. A record that no commit accepts yet awaits its yes: the gate reports a note and never a finding, since a gate is not red for waiting on a person; an `Accepted:` id that names no record is a note too — deleted since, or misspelt. The receipt shows that an audit was declared and accounted for, not that it was run.
6. <a id="r6"></a>**Enforcement.** Module `const-decision-records` guards `type`, `stakes` and `decider` of every document in this territory — this policy's id is named in its dial `record_policies` in `constitution.yaml` — at write time when a session loads the plugin, and by its scan; the rest of the envelope is module `const-meta-types`'. Module `const-catalogs` guards the name, the id and a new item's number, free in the working tree, the same two ways, and every collision between items that exist in the tree it scans, in its scan alone; module `const-referencing` guards links, anchors and, in its scan alone, the `dec-` mentions outside Markdown; `scripts/check-carry-lists.py` guards the carry list's shape and the receipt. The body's shape, whether a ruling is carried, and the yes itself are review, as rule 2 says. The twelve traces of the style defaults are held by module `const-styling` in its scan alone, which runs no hook. The grade, the sentence length and the constructions of the prose are held by module `const-language`, after a write and in its scan; a threshold or a list an accepted record fails is met by the wording edits of rule 14; what no such edit meets is declared as a departure of that gate's dial in `constitution.yaml` over the record's path or a number range that holds it, with its reason, as a trace of a style default is. The list-handles scan of module `const-interaction` and the marker scan of module `const-questions` read documents of this kind too and have no such dial: a plain list item in an accepted record takes a bold lead, and a marker it quotes takes a code span. An accepted record is never taken out of any gate's territory.
7. <a id="r7"></a>**Deletion.** No record has to be deleted. An `inactive` record whose ruling is carried binds nothing, and no rule depends on it ([kernel §5](../../kernel/CONVENTIONS.md#k5)); it is kept as evidence of the options and reasoning. Whether anything should still reference it ([kernel §1](../../kernel/CONVENTIONS.md#k1)) is the decider's judgment, not a count of links. Deleting one is routine and is one commit: every link to it is removed, the record is removed, and the commit message names the record and calls the change a deletion. Inside another record, in a frozen comparison and in a done line of the plan, the link becomes the deleted record's title in plain words — never its id, so nothing in the tree points at what is gone — which inside a record is a reference-form migration in the kernel's sense, the ruling untouched; in a comparison it is an edit the freeze of [rule 7 of plc-0005-research](plc-0005-research.md#r7) allows by name, and in the plan a routine edit; the commit names it. An `active` record is never deleted.
8. <a id="r8"></a>**Template.** A record is written from the kit's own template, `templates/decision-record.md`, the one named for this kind in `const-locations.templates`: the shape the record skill of module `const-decision-records` ships to every consumer, with what rules 2, 9, 11 and 12 add — the carry list, the handles, the Applied section, the wording of the status and of the Choice — which are the kit's rules and no module's. The template carries every section and wording those rules require, and a change to any of them changes the template in the same change.
9. <a id="r9"></a>**Sections.** Shape one, when a real choice existed: Context, Options, Recommendation, Choice, Ruling, Applied where the record says what its creating commit did, Carry list. Shape two: Context, Ruling, Reasoning, Carry list. A record that answers several questions has one numbered section per question, each with its context, its lettered options, one recommendation with its reasoning — one sentence is enough — the choice, and the ruling opening with its handle; an objection the session keeps against the choice stands in the record. The title is one line that states the ruling, never the topic, and the H1 repeats it.
10. <a id="r10"></a>**Items and handles.** A ruling item is any sentence that binds after the yes, wherever in the record it stands — a default the session took and left open to override included; the test is whether the blind reader of rule 2 would need it to answer. Handles run in order, contiguous and unique in the record, tables included; a key of another document is written qualified — "message R4 of the module". One kind of home per handle — links, "history only" or "pending": an item partly binding and partly history is split in two, or carried with its history written after the link; handles sharing one home may be grouped in one entry; the entry form is the gate's, `R1 — …`.
11. <a id="r11"></a>**Status.** A record is `inactive` once every item has its home in the sense of rule 2 — stated in a current-state document, marked "history only" because it binds nothing, or "pending" in the plan, the write-head that holds intent not yet real until it is ([kernel §2](../../kernel/CONVENTIONS.md#k2)): the kit reads the kernel's "applied" so, a pending item's ruling being that the work is planned — and `active` only while an item has none. A record not applied in its creating commit because it grows while its change is made — a module's rulings record, a record whose slots are decided one per message — is born `active` and set `inactive` in the commit that names its yes, or that ends its review, since until then an item may still be added; one applied in its creating commit is born `inactive`, as the kernel says, and a `product` record, having no window, cannot grow and follows the first sentence alone.
12. <a id="r12"></a>**Body facts.** The body says "the decider"; the Choice names where and when the pick was made — "in chat on <date>" — since a yes given in chat reaches git only with the commit that names it; a commit hash appears for an audit's pin or for where git holds a deleted file that is no record — a deleted record is named by its title, rule 7 — and nowhere else ([requirement A5.6](../requirements.md#a5-6)).
13. <a id="r13"></a>**Reversal mechanics.** A reversing record names the reversed item by record and handle, and the reversal itself is carried "history only". The reversed record is not touched: a pointer to the reversing record added to an accepted record is new content, not the mechanical edit of [kernel §5](../../kernel/CONVENTIONS.md#k5), and the kernel's precedence clock already orders the two.
14. <a id="r14"></a>**Earlier records.** An accepted record is never edited to fit a later rule, whatever the rule, beyond the wording edits this rule lists; where a rule of this policy applies from a number on, the rule names the threshold, and records below it are not brought to it. A wording edit ([kernel §5](../../kernel/CONVENTIONS.md#k5)) leaves the record's meaning as it stands: it changes no ruling, no fact and no number, removes or alters no link, anchor, code span or existing handle, and changes no word of a quotation. It is one of these kinds and no other. (a) A sentence split where one independent clause ends and the next begins. It is never made at a dash that closes a pair of dashes, and never between the items of a list unless each item is an independent clause. The dash, semicolon, colon or comma there becomes a full stop. The next word takes a capital unless it is code or a link, the first word of a handle such as "rule 4 of …" included. A joining word there — *and*, *but*, *so*, *while* — is dropped. A clause whose subject was shared takes it again, as the same noun or a pronoun for it. (b) A list item that opens with no list handle has its opening words set in bold. At most its words up to and including the first comma, colon, semicolon or full stop are set so. No word is added, dropped or moved. (c) A word that the `simpler` list of module `const-language` pairs with a plain word is replaced by that plain word, an article before it made to agree. (d) A quotation, or a word mentioned as a word, that holds a phrase of that module's `forbidden` or `simpler` list or a marker of module `const-questions` is put whole in a code span, its words unchanged and its quotation marks outside the span. (e) A list written inside a sentence is set as a bulleted list under its lead. Such a list is items that follow a colon, or the bold lead of a list item, separated by semicolons. Each item becomes one bullet, its words and its closing semicolon or full stop unchanged. The bullets are nested under the list item the list stood in, if it stood in one. The text after the last item becomes a paragraph of its own. A bullet that opens with no list handle takes kind (b). A wording edit is routine and is made only in a change that has a carry audit, whose readers read each such edit against these kinds; the commit names it a wording edit and names the records it edits.
15. <a id="r15"></a>**Other stakes.** Every record of the kit so far is `process`. An `architecture` record's review, which [kernel §5](../../kernel/CONVENTIONS.md#k5) leaves the project to define, is the two passes of step 6 of "Developing a module" in [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md), with every finding fixed or justified: a fresh reader holding the record, the current-state documents it changes and this policy, never the authoring reasoning, and then the carry audit. The review is complete when the decider says so — in chat, or, from rollout Phase D, in the pull request, where rule 5 puts the yes — and the commit that names it carries the two trailers of rule 5 as the receipt — so the correction window of rule 5 closes where [kernel §5](../../kernel/CONVENTIONS.md#k5) closes it for this stakes, at the review's end. A reviewer other than the decider replaces that first pass once the kit has a second human, by an amendment of this rule under [rule 3 of plc-0001-root-policy](plc-0001-root-policy.md#r3). A `product` record, which the kernel gives neither a yes nor a review, is complete when committed and has no window: the carry audit is run on the commit that holds it, and a finding is fixed in the documents or by a further record, never by editing the record beyond the mechanical edits of [kernel §5](../../kernel/CONVENTIONS.md#k5); the commit that holds the fixes carries the `Carry-Audit:` trailer alone, and the gate's note that the record awaits a yes is the honest limit of a gate that does not read stakes.
16. <a id="r16"></a>**Style.** Documents of this kind follow the style guides the dial `guides` of module `const-styling` names in `constitution.yaml`, and depart from a default only where that module's dial `departures` names a departure for their territory, with its reason ([rule 9 of plc-0001-root-policy](plc-0001-root-policy.md#r9)); the traces of the defaults are held as the enforcement rule says, the rest is review. A trace of a default in an accepted record is removed by a wording edit of rule 14 where one removes it; where none does, it is declared by a departure over the record's path or a number range that holds it, and never by taking accepted records out of the scan's territory (decisions [dec-0045-frozen-documents-stay-in-the-style-scan](../decisions/dec-0045-frozen-documents-stay-in-the-style-scan.md) and [dec-0048-frozen-documents-take-wording-edits](../decisions/dec-0048-frozen-documents-take-wording-edits.md)).
