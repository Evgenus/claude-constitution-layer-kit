# Rollout-plan policy

Governs the document kind *rollout plan*: the phases of the kit's rollout and their sequence, the kit's own task-record. Territory under this policy's id in `constitution.yaml`.

## Rules

1. <a id="r1"></a>**Type.** `task-record`, `active` while a phase remains, `inactive` when the last phase is reached — step 11 done, rule 7 ([kernel §2](../../kernel/CONVENTIONS.md#k2)); a stretch with no commits in its scope is stalled drift.
2. <a id="r2"></a>**Content.** Phases, steps, their items and status, and sequence only. A requirement belongs to the requirements document, a ruling already made to its record and to the current-state text that carries it; neither is stated here. Intent not yet real that a "pending" carry entry points at is stated in its step until the step closes (rule 6).
3. <a id="r3"></a>**Change.** Reordering or rewriting phases is a `process`-stakes decision ([law 9 of CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md#law-9)); ticking an item done, with its step's row and the row `all` of the status matrix in the same change, is routine (rule 10).
4. <a id="r4"></a>**Anchors and numbers.** Every phase carries an explicit anchor `phase-<letter>` and every step `s<n>`. Steps are numbered once across the phases and keep their number; a step added later stands after the one it follows as `<n>a`, `<n>b`, with the anchor to match. Every item of a step carries an explicit anchor `s<n>-<m>` and opens with the bold handle `<n>.<m>`. Every list line under a step, outside a fenced block, is one of its items, and an item is one line. Items are numbered within their step in ascending order, from 1, and keep their number; a number once used is not used again, so an item removed leaves a gap. An item added later at the end of its step takes a number above the highest; one added between two items stands after the one it follows as `<n>.<m>a`, `<n>.<m>b`, with the anchor to match.
5. <a id="r5"></a>**Status and done.** Every item opens, after its handle, with one mark. ✅ is done. ⏳ is open: to be done in the plan's order, which is the order of its steps and, within a step, only the order its items' own words set, and not before a date its text sets for the work to start. 💤 is waiting: started only when the signal, the condition or the ask its text names comes, which may never come; a date is no such condition. An item is one piece of work the plan names, or a group of pieces the plan names together; a piece or a group whose parts would carry different marks is split into items, one mark each. A step with no items is its own one item, its mark right after its anchor; a step with items carries no mark of its own. An item ticked done says, in one of its sentences, its date, its record — the record's title in plain words once the record is deleted ([rule 7 of plc-0004-decision-records](plc-0004-decision-records.md#r7)) — and "the yes given", or its date and the words "a routine change". An item already done when the status matrix came in need not take this form, nor shed what it holds. Every done item links its record, or the document or the module a routine change concerns, or, once the record is deleted, the current-state document that carries its ruling, which the change that deletes the record links: that link is its proof. The evidence lives in the record, the module document or, for a routine change, its commit, never here.
6. <a id="r6"></a>**Pending home.** This is the document a carry list's "pending" entry points at ([rule 2 of plc-0004-decision-records](plc-0004-decision-records.md#r2)): a step so named closes only with its intent carried into the current-state documents, in the same change, and the note the carry-lists gate prints for it is answered by the next audit in full.
7. <a id="r7"></a>**Close.** The plan is `inactive` when step 11 is done. The last phase describes the steady state and holds no rule: what it points at lives in the intent page and the policies.
8. <a id="r8"></a>**Enforcement.** The envelope by module [`const-meta-types`](../requirements.md#const-meta-types); links and anchors by module [`const-referencing`](../requirements.md#const-referencing); `scripts/check-carry-lists.py` refuses this document as a home, so a ruling stated only here is found by the carry audit. The kit's `scripts/check-rollout-status.py`, an entry of the gate list the kit's CI runs with `--self-test` as its fixture, holds the matrix and the items. Of the matrix it holds one table with the matrix's header, before the first phase, in the form rule 10 gives; one row for each step and none for another, in the plan's order, and the row `all` last; and each row's phase, counts, percent and status, as rule 10 derives them. Of the steps and items it holds each step under a phase anchor, its number once; every list line of a step, outside a fenced block, an item in the form of rule 4, with one mark and words after it, its handle and anchor matching its step and its numbers ascending; no mark on a step with items and one on a step without; and a link on each done item's line. Whether a mark matches its item's words, whether a link is its proof, a done item's words and a row's title are review. Other content is review, the traces of the style defaults excepted. The twelve traces of the style defaults are held by module [`const-styling`](../requirements.md#const-styling) in its scan alone, which runs no hook. The grade, the sentence length and the constructions of the prose are held by module [`const-language`](../requirements.md#const-language), after a write and in its scan. The length in characters is held by module [`const-doc-budgets`](../requirements.md#const-doc-budgets), after a write and in its scan, against the ceiling its dial `ceilings` in `constitution.yaml` sets for documents of this kind.
9. <a id="r9"></a>**Style.** Documents of this kind follow the style guides the dial `guides` of module [`const-styling`](../requirements.md#const-styling) names in `constitution.yaml`, and depart from a default only where that module's dial `departures` names a departure for their territory, with its reason ([rule 9 of plc-0001-root-policy](plc-0001-root-policy.md#r9)); the traces of the defaults are held as the enforcement rule says, the rest is review.
10. <a id="r10"></a>**Status matrix.** The plan holds a status matrix before its first phase: a table of one row per step in the plan's order, under the header row `| Step | Title | Phase | Status | Done | Open | Waiting | % done |`. A row's cells are the step's handle linked to its anchor, `[s<n>](#s<n>)`; a short title; its phase's capital letter; its status; the counts of its done, open and waiting items; and its percent done. A last row, `all` in its handle and phase cells, holds the sums, its percent and status derived by the same rules. The counts are of items, whatever each holds, so the percent measures items, not effort. The percent is `done * 100 // (done + open)`, a whole number rounded down, so a waiting item counts in neither part; it is `n/a` where done and open are both 0. The status is ✅ when no item is open or waiting, ⏳ when an item is open and none is done, 🔄 when an item is done and another is open, and 💤 when no item is open and one is waiting.
