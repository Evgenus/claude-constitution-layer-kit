# Root policy

Governs the document kind *policy*: a document that states the rules of one document kind, this one included. Territory under this policy's id in `constitution.yaml`.

This document is the root of governance in this repository, per [kernel §3](../../kernel/CONVENTIONS.md#k3): its territory, declared under its id in the kit's `constitution.yaml`, is where policies live, and this file lies inside it. A document is a policy because it sits here. Nothing declares itself one.

## Rules

1. <a id="r1"></a>**Home.** Every policy of the kit is a Markdown document in this policy's territory, carrying the kernel envelope ([kernel §1](../../kernel/CONVENTIONS.md#k1)) with `type: current-state`.
2. <a id="r2"></a>**Territory.** A policy claims paths only through its dials namespace, keyed by its id, in `constitution.yaml`. A policy with no namespace claims nothing.
3. <a id="r3"></a>**Creation and change.** Adding, removing, or re-scoping a policy, and amending a rule of one, is a `process`-stakes decision ([kernel §4](../../kernel/CONVENTIONS.md#k4)): a record in `docs/decisions/` and one human yes. Carrying an already-ruled item into a policy, its ruling unchanged, is a repair and routine (decision [dec-0028-carrying-a-ruling-is-routine](../decisions/dec-0028-carrying-a-ruling-is-routine.md)); a mechanical edit — a typo, a reference-form migration — is routine too. When module `const-locations` is installed, its creation command writes the document here — from the policy template when `const-locations.templates` names one for this policy, with the envelope alone otherwise; the body follows the template's shape either way. Until then, by hand from the template. The template is `templates/policy.md`; a rule of this policy that changes the shape changes the template in the same change.
4. <a id="r4"></a>**Body.** A policy's body is prose: what kind it governs, its rules as numbered items, and the kernel sections it relies on by link. It opens with the sentence the template writes — "Governs the document kind *<kind>*: …" — its title is "<Kind> policy — <one clause>", the kind named in full or by its short name — "Intent policy" for the intent page, and "Root policy — <one clause>" for this policy, whose kind is *policy* — and every rule opens with a bold name. It never restates the kernel ([law 1 of CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md#law-1)) and never lists the documents it governs — the territory under its id is the enumeration. A policy states its own rules itself; a citation of another policy points at where a rule about the other's kind lives, and a clause the citing policy also states is its own, the link being provenance. This policy, where every chain of governance ends ([kernel §3](../../kernel/CONVENTIONS.md#k3)), relies on no rule of any other policy and cites none.
5. <a id="r5"></a>**One kind.** A policy governs exactly one document kind ([kernel §3](../../kernel/CONVENTIONS.md#k3)). A document of a kind no policy governs gets its policy first, by rule 3.
6. <a id="r6"></a>**Number.** Policies are a numbered kind: id and filename stem are one name, `plc-<nnnn>-<slug>`, the slug naming the kind governed and never the kit; prefix, width and territory are an entry of `const-catalogs.catalogs` in `constitution.yaml`, its territory this policy's own by a YAML alias. A new policy takes one more than the highest number the catalog has held, deleted policies included, provisional until it lands on main ([requirement A13.1](../requirements.md#a13-1), [A13.3](../requirements.md#a13-3)). A policy's id is also its namespace key in `constitution.yaml` (rule 2), so the key is written in the same form; a `plc-` name written outside Markdown — a key, the `root_policy` path, `record_policies` — matches a pattern of the `mentions` dial of module `const-referencing`, whose scan reports one that is not an id a document the scan covers carries.
7. <a id="r7"></a>**Anchors.** Every rule carries an explicit anchor `r<n>` inside its item, before the bold name, as the laws of `CLAUDE.md` do ([requirement A5.5](../requirements.md#a5-5)). From another document a rule is cited as a link to its anchor, "rule n of <policy id>" being the link's text or the words that carry it; inside its own policy, "rule n" is enough. A rule keeps its number and anchor for life, since accepted records cite rules by number and are not edited to fit later text: a rule withdrawn keeps its number and its item says so, with a link to the record that withdrew it; a new rule takes the next number.
8. <a id="r8"></a>**Enforcement.** Every policy carries a rule so named, stating which module guards which field of its kind — at write time, in its scan, or both, the split named where the two differ — and what stays judgment, held by review ([law 5 of CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md#law-5)). For the kind this policy governs: the envelope by module `const-meta-types`; the name, the id and a new item's number, free in the working tree, by module `const-catalogs` at write time and in its scan, and every collision between items that exist in the tree it scans, in its scan alone; links and anchors by module `const-referencing`, and the `plc-` mentions outside Markdown by it in its scan alone; whether a body states one kind's rules, and whether a rule is stated in full, is review. The twelve traces of the style defaults are held by module `const-styling` in its scan alone, which runs no hook. The grade, the sentence length and the constructions of the prose are held by module `const-language`, after a write and in its scan.
9. <a id="r9"></a>**Style.** Every policy carries a rule so named, stating that its kind's documents follow the style guides — the documents the dial `guides` of module `const-styling` names in `constitution.yaml` — and depart from a default only where that module's dial `departures` names a departure for their territory, with its reason. The rule names no departure, since the dial is their one home. Documents of its kind that a rule of its policy freezes — an accepted record, a frozen comparison — stand as written, and a trace of a default in them is declared by a departure over their number range or their path, never by an edit and never by taking them out of the scan's territory. Policies are documents of this kind and follow the guides the same way. A departure is declared in the dials file, never marked in a document; a default is added to a guide through the decision path ([kernel §4](../../kernel/CONVENTIONS.md#k4)), never to a policy of another kind.
