## 1. The carrier of the style defaults ([requirement A9.1](../requirements.md#a9-1))

How "one style-defaults policy that templates inherit" can exist in a repository whose documents resolve to policies by territory, and what a module may ship of it:

- a1. **What "policy" can mean here.** A policy governs exactly one document kind by its territory, and a document in the root policy's territory with no namespace "is a policy that claims nothing" ([kernel §3](../../kernel/CONVENTIONS.md#k3)); [rule 2 of plc-0001-root-policy](../policies/plc-0001-root-policy.md#r2) says the same of the kit's. A style-defaults policy governs no kind: it rules on the content of every kind — diagrams, lists, tables, prose — and the only kernel object that is a policy without a territory is the claims-nothing one. What binds a document to it is not resolution but citation: each kind's template, or the shape rule of each kind's policy, says that the defaults apply. That is the mechanism the matrix calls "templates inherit". Under [rule 5 of plc-0001-root-policy](../policies/plc-0001-root-policy.md#r5), "A policy governs exactly one document kind", the kit's own copy would be a policy of zero kinds: a reading the decider makes, or an amendment under [rule 3 of plc-0001-root-policy](../policies/plc-0001-root-policy.md#r3) at `process` stakes.
- a2. **What stays with the kinds.** Shape rules already live per kind: the sections of a record ([rule 9 of plc-0004-decision-records](../policies/plc-0004-decision-records.md#r9)), the shape of a comparison ([rule 5 of plc-0005-research](../policies/plc-0005-research.md#r5)), the body of a policy ([rule 4 of plc-0001-root-policy](../policies/plc-0001-root-policy.md#r4)), and E5 measures them held — 43 records of which 29 carry an Options section and 30 a Ruling section, 16 a Carry list (the rule applies from record 0030), 9 comparisons of which all 9 carry a summary. The style defaults are therefore what is shared *across* kinds and never a kind's sections; that line is what keeps [law 1 of CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md#law-1) between the style policy and the kind policies.
- a3. **How a template inherits.** The command of `const-locations` copies a template's body verbatim ("every other field and the whole body are copied as they stand for the writer to fill", its rule L-d), and the kit's templates open with an instruction paragraph the writer deletes (`templates/policy.md`: "Delete this paragraph"). An inheritance sentence lives in that paragraph, or in the template's opening italic note, and costs nothing in the documents born from it; the four templates the kit names in `const-locations.templates` — policy, comparison, module document, record — would each carry one line. A template that departs from a default carries the marker of section 2 at the place it departs.
- a4. **Candidate A: a document the consumer creates from the module's template.** The module ships the policy's text as a file — the precedent is `const-decision-records`, whose skill ships `record-template.md`, and the kit's own record template being that file plus the kit's additions ([rule 8 of plc-0004-decision-records](../policies/plc-0004-decision-records.md#r8)) — and the consumer creates the document in its root policy's territory through the command of `const-locations` with the root policy as its kind when that module is installed, by hand from the file otherwise; a numbered kind's stem comes from `const-catalogs` when installed. The consumer's copy is the home of every default accreted later ([requirement A9.3](../requirements.md#a9-3)) and is versioned by the consumer. Mechanism class: a document; maturity: the pattern of every kit kind; cost: one file in the module, one policy in the consumer.
- a5. **Candidate B: a skill.** The default invocation — description always in the listing, the body loaded on invocation; "Put the key use case first: the combined `description` and `when_to_use` text is truncated at 1,536 characters in the skill listing" ([skills](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/skills)). The description phrased by the activities that precede a deviation: about to draw a diagram, about to choose between prose, a list and a table, about to write a formula, about to write or edit a template. The body carries the three founding entries with their contrastive examples, the deviation marker, the inheritance sentence and the accretion rule. On a bare repository with the kernel alone ([law 3 of CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md#law-3)) this is the only carrier that works with zero configuration. Mechanism class: skill prose; cost: the description's characters per turn.
- a6. **Candidate C: a `SessionStart` line.** "For most events, Claude Code writes stdout to the debug log and doesn't show it in the transcript. The exceptions are `UserPromptSubmit`, `UserPromptExpansion`, `SessionStart`, and `PostModelSwitch`, where Claude Code adds plain-text stdout as context that Claude can see and act on" ([hooks](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/hooks)); the carrier of `const-interaction` (a1 of [res-0008-const-interaction](res-0008-const-interaction.md)). The three founding entries fit in about 250 characters. But a style decision has a trigger — a diagram, a table, a formula about to be written — and the reasoning of a3 of [res-0007-const-questions](res-0007-const-questions.md) applies: a rule with a trigger belongs in a skill's description; the always-on line pays every session for a rule that most sessions never reach. Not recommended; recorded as the fallback if a consumer reports the skill not reached.
- a7. **Candidate D: a section of the consumer's root policy.** The root policy governs the kind *policy* and "relies on no rule of any other policy and cites none" ([rule 4 of plc-0001-root-policy](../policies/plc-0001-root-policy.md#r4)); rules about diagrams in every document are not rules about policies, and a module cannot edit a consumer's document at all ([kernel §6](../../kernel/CONVENTIONS.md#k6)). Rejected.
- a8. **The two copies.** The skill states the founding entries; the consumer's policy states them too, since "A policy states its own rules itself" ([rule 4 of plc-0001-root-policy](../policies/plc-0001-root-policy.md#r4)). Three lines exist twice: the consumer's copy binds and grows; the skill's is the floor for a repository that has no policy yet and the teaching at the moment of writing, kept current by the module's version bumps. This is the same cost a10 of [res-0008-const-interaction](res-0008-const-interaction.md) priced for a consumer's copy of the reply rules, smaller here because the text is three sentences, and the drift is the consumer's accretion by design.
- a9. **No dial.** The specification's "no dial at founding" holds: the consumer's policy is the calibration surface, as the `templates` dial is for `const-locations` — nothing in the module's namespace but, should the scan of section 2 ship, its `territories`.
- a10. **What no carrier reaches.** A session that draws three boxes in a fence without judging it "a diagram" invokes nothing (the pattern of a8 of [res-0007-const-questions](res-0007-const-questions.md)); the scan of section 2 catches the signatures it knows after the fact, and the rest is review.

Mechanism class: a4 a document, a5 skill prose, a6 a hook carrying prose, a7 a consumer's edit. Maturity: all documented; a4 and a5 the shape of shipped modules. Cost: a5 the cheapest that has a trigger.

**Recommendation: B for the module's carrier and A for the document — one skill whose description is phrased by the activities before a deviation and whose body carries the founding entries, the marker, the inheritance sentence and the accretion rule; and the policy's text shipped as a template file the consumer's own style policy is born from, in the consumer's root territory, claiming nothing; the inheritance wording one sentence in each template's deletable paragraph; no `SessionStart` line; CONFIRMED as advisory.** For the decider: whether the kit's own style policy — the next number of the `plc` catalog — stands under [rule 5 of plc-0001-root-policy](../policies/plc-0001-root-policy.md#r5) as a claims-nothing policy by reading, or the rule is amended to name the case; and whether the inheritance sentence lives in the templates' deletable paragraphs or in the shape rule of each kind's policy.

## 2. Deviation marking and its gate-lite ([requirement A9.1](../requirements.md#a9-1))

- b1. **What a deviation is.** A template or a document using a form the defaults rule out where a default exists: a PlantUML block, a non-trivial drawing in a fence, a formula where pseudocode was due; after accretion, whatever the consumer's policy adds. The matrix asks for a one-line reason, and the specification for templates to "state only deviations".
- b2. **Marker candidates.** A: an HTML comment on the line before the deviating element, `<!-- style: <the default deviated from> — <reason> -->`. B: a visible italic line in the same place. C: an envelope field, `style_deviations`, a list; the kernel allows added fields ([kernel §1](../../kernel/CONVENTIONS.md#k1)). D: an attribute in the fence's info string, which reaches only fenced deviations. A renders as nothing on GitHub, GitLab and in an editor's preview — GitLab's style guide says "To embed comments in Markdown, use standard HTML comments that are not rendered when published" — and a model reading the raw file sees it as it sees B; the scan of `const-referencing` masks HTML comments, so nothing inside is linted as a link; the kit already relies on HTML in Markdown for its anchors (E4: 351 `<a id=` anchors in the scoped tree). B is visible in rendered output beside the thing it excuses, a signal to the human reader at the cost of a line of noise in every rendering. C detaches the reason from its place: a document with two diagrams needs the field to name which. D is too narrow.
- b3. **What is mechanically detectable.** Two things, both deterministic: a marker whose reason is empty after the dash; and a *known signature* with no marker on the line before it — a fence whose info string is `plantuml` or `puml`, a line beginning `@startuml`, a line beginning `$$`, a fence whose info string is `math`. A deviation with no signature — a drawing in a plain fence, a table where a list was due, an aside set off as a callout — is judgment, and the check's message may claim no more than "no marked signature without a reason" ([law 5 of CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md#law-5)). E1 shows both halves in Vale's terms: an `existence` rule at `scope: raw` fires on the fence and on `@startuml`, and a `script` rule tells a fence with a reason line from one without; E4 shows the kit's tree carrying none of the signatures today.
- b4. **False positives.** The documents that quote the syntax: the style policy itself, the module's document, this comparison. A quote inside a code span is masked by any reader that masks spans; a quote as a nested fence is a fence at line start, which the check must read, so it fires — the same shape as e2 of [res-0007-const-questions](res-0007-const-questions.md), where five of eight marker hits were the documents defining the marker. The remedy is the consumer's negated pattern in the module's `territories` dial, the pattern of `const-questions`, which on the kit takes out three files. The kit's three fences holding box-drawing characters (E4) are all one directory tree — the plugin skeleton of the module template and its two copies — trivial by the examples of section 3, so no marker is due and no signature fires.
- b5. **Write time or scan.** The reasoning of e4 of [res-0007-const-questions](res-0007-const-questions.md) holds unchanged: a hook on `Write` would block the template that defines the marker and the module document that explains it; the moment the row speaks of is a template or document delivered, so the check is `--scan` only, over an own `territories` dial, run on demand and in CI. The precedent is shipped: `check-list-handles --scan` of `const-interaction`, "no hook mode" (R3 of [dec-0042-interaction-module-rulings](../decisions/dec-0042-interaction-module-rulings.md)), whose hook function refuses with a message of its own, "this check has no hook mode: it scans a tree and judges no single write".
- b6. **Cost.** One Python entry point on the shared code with one check and a handful of signatures; a red→green fixture per signature and one for the empty reason; a finding per line with the exact fix; a `territories` dial. Whether a mechanism this small earns its fixtures is the decider's, who has twice shipped such a slice when cheap (b5 of [res-0008-const-interaction](res-0008-const-interaction.md)).

Mechanism class: a deterministic scan, gate-lite; maturity: trivial on the shared code, proved in another tool's terms by E1; cost: b6, plus the negated patterns the consumer writes for the documents that quote the syntax.

**Recommendation: marker A, an HTML comment `<!-- style: <default> — <reason> -->` on the line before the deviating element; the gate-lite CONFIRMED and build — one `--scan`-only script, `check-style-deviations`, over an own `territories` dial, red on a known signature with no marker on the line before and on a marker with an empty reason, never on anything else, one script serving this row and the next.** For the decider: A or B as the marker; whether the block-math signatures of section 3 join the PlantUML ones at founding or wait for a consumer's formula.

## 3. Diagram defaults and their gate-lite ([requirement A9.2](../requirements.md#a9-2))

- c1. **GitHub renders Mermaid natively and nothing else of this kind.** "You can create diagrams in Markdown using four different syntaxes: mermaid, geoJSON, topoJSON, and ASCII STL"; "To create a Mermaid diagram, add Mermaid syntax inside a fenced code block with the mermaid language identifier"; rendering "is available in GitHub Issues, GitHub Discussions, pull requests, wikis, and Markdown files" (t7, creating diagrams). PlantUML is not on the list: a PlantUML block on GitHub is a code block.
- c2. **GitLab renders Mermaid; PlantUML and Kroki need a server an administrator enables.** "GitLab supports Mermaid version 11"; "PlantUML integration is enabled on GitLab.com. To make PlantUML available in GitLab Self-Managed installation of GitLab, a GitLab administrator must enable it"; "To make Kroki available in GitLab, a GitLab administrator must enable it" (t8, Markdown page). GitLab's own style guide: "Mermaid (recommended)", "Mermaid is the recommended diagramming tool, but it is not suitable for all situations", with draw.io as the GUI fallback for layouts Mermaid cannot make legible.
- c3. **Editors.** "VS Code's built-in Markdown preview renders Mermaid diagrams in mermaid fenced code blocks" (t14). PlantUML "is a highly versatile tool" in Java whose FAQ names the Graphviz versions known to work and recommends "a local server on your own network if you plan to generate diagrams with sensitive information" (t6): a renderer to install and version, against a JavaScript library the page already loads.
- c4. **Claude Code.** In the 210 documentation pages the only occurrence of the word is an example output style — "When explaining code, architecture, or data flow, start with a Mermaid diagram showing the structure, then explain in prose" ([output styles](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/output-styles)); the desktop page says nothing of diagrams, and the changelog notes only that prompts render Markdown in the transcript. Whether a Mermaid fence renders anywhere in Claude Code is not documented. What follows: in the sessions the kit governs a diagram is read as its source, by the model and by a reader at a terminal alike, so the syntax must read as text — which Mermaid's and PlantUML's both do — and it renders where the document lands, which favours the one GitHub, GitLab and the editor render without setup.
- c5. **Where Mermaid stops, so the one-line justification can name it.** PlantUML's home page lists, beyond the UML set, "JSON … YAML … EBNF … Regex … Network diagram (nwdiag) … Salt (Wireframe …) … Archimate … SDL … Ditaa … Gantt … Chronology" and, among UML, object, component, deployment and timing diagrams (t6). Mermaid's documented syntax list has flowchart, sequence, class, state, entity-relationship, user journey, Gantt, pie, quadrant, requirement, use case, git graph, C4 (marked experimental), mindmap, timeline, ZenUML, Sankey, XY, block, packet, kanban, architecture, radar and more (t5). A timing diagram, a wireframe, a deployment diagram with nested nodes, an EBNF railroad, a network diagram: each is one clause of the kind the row asks for. The reverse gap exists too — quadrant, Sankey, XY, kanban, radar are Mermaid's — and needs no justification.
- c6. **Public guides on drawing in text.** Kubernetes, in its style guide's Do and Don't table: don't "Use multi-line blocks of code to create diagrams, flowcharts, or other illustrations" (t11), and its diagram guide is a Mermaid guide; GitLab, c2; Google's pages read carry no diagram rule; Microsoft's none. The strictest public rule is Kubernetes' outright ban on drawing in a fence; the row's "trivial-only" is the kit's softer line, and the tree shows why: the module template's plugin skeleton is a directory tree in box-drawing characters, which no diagram tool draws better.
- c7. **ASCII art is honestly ungateable; the examples define it.** A fence holding box-drawing characters or arrows is a signal, not a verdict: E4 finds 3 such fences in the scoped tree, every one the directory tree, and 0 fences with arrow drawings. Four contrastive pairs, which the module's document would carry: trivial — a directory tree; a one-line chain `a -> b -> c`; two columns aligned by spaces; a bracketed range on one line. Not trivial — boxes joined by arrows on several lines; lifelines with messages between them; anything a reader must redraw to change one node; anything with more than one path through it. A scan may list fences holding box-drawing characters as notes, never as findings.
- c8. **Math renders in the web UI and nowhere else the kit's readers sit.** GitHub "supports LaTeX formatted math within Markdown" through MathJax, inline with `$` delimiters, as a block with `$$` or a fence whose info string is `math` (t7, mathematical expressions); GitLab renders "Math written in LaTeX syntax … with KaTeX" and "only the first 1000 inline math instances" (t8); Kubernetes turns it on per page with `math: true` (t11). Vale "ignores … Math: `$$…$$` blocks and `$x^2$` spans" (t2, Markdown page): a formula is invisible to the prose linter of [`const-language`](../requirements.md#const-language). Google's page on mathematical notation formats operators with HTML entities and variables in italics, and defers: "If you're using a third-party tool to display complex math, follow that tool's formatting guidance". Pseudocode in a fence is read the same in a diff, a terminal, a model's context and a page, its language tag names it, and every linter treats it as code. That is why the row prefers it, and the reasoning belongs in the policy's one line.
- c9. **Detecting a formula.** A line beginning `$$` and a fence whose info string is `math` are signatures: 0 in the kit (E4). The inline form is not: 23 `$…$`-shaped spans on the kit's tree, every one a shell variable or a placeholder (E4) — the ambiguity Vale's Markdown page resolves with Pandoc's delimiter rules and the kit's documents would trip at every `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}`.
- c10. **Detecting PlantUML.** A fence whose info string is `plantuml` or `puml`, and `@startuml` at a line's start anywhere, since GitLab's page says the delimiters "aren't required" inside a `plantuml` block but the bare form is what a PlantUML user writes elsewhere. E1 catches both with one rule; the reason line beside them with a second.

Mechanism class: the three entries advisory prose in the skill and the consumer's policy; the PlantUML and block-math signatures a deterministic scan, the one of section 2. Maturity: the rendering facts documented on the platforms' own pages today; the scan trivial. Cost: nothing for the entries; the scan's cost is section 2's.

**Recommendation: CONFIRMED as advisory for the three founding entries — Mermaid by default, PlantUML with the one-line reason naming the missing capability from the list of c5, ASCII art trivial-only by the four pairs of c7, pseudocode over formulas with c8's reasoning — and the gate-lite CONFIRMED and build as the PlantUML signatures of c10 in the scan of section 2; ASCII art honestly ungateable and said so in the module's document.** For the decider: whether the block-math signatures of c9 join the scan at founding.

## 5. Vale graduation ([requirement A9.4](../requirements.md#a9-4))

- e1. **The state of Vale.** `vale --version` prints `vale version 3.19.0` locally; the latest release is v3.23.0 of 2026-09-25 with binaries for Linux, macOS and Windows on two architectures. The organisation is `vale-cli`: a request to the old `errata-ai/vale` address is redirected there today, and the package library the Packages page links is `vale-cli/packages` — the specification's rename claim stands. Pinning stands too: "To hold a package at a known version, give the release URL in place of the name", with the example `https://github.com/vale-cli/Google/releases/download/v0.7.0/Google.zip` (t2, Packages page).
- e2. **How a Markdown style rule becomes a Vale rule.** One YAML file per rule in a style directory under `StylesPath`, `extends` naming the check — `existence` for tokens or a raw regular expression, `substitution`, `capitalization`, `script` for logic in Tengo, "compiled once with the rule and run once per block of its scope", stored in `<StylesPath>/config/scripts` (t2, script page). A rule's `scope` selects blocks by parts — `heading`, `heading.h4`, `list`, `table.cell`, `blockquote`, `raw` (t2, scopes page). Vale's Markdown reader "By default … ignores: Indented blocks … Fenced blocks … Code spans … Math … URLs" (t2, Markdown page): a rule about a fence needs `scope: raw`, which E1 shows — at `scope: text` the rule caught the bare `@startuml` and not the fence.
- e3. **How it ships.** A package is "a style, a configuration file, or both, published as an archive", named in `Packages` by library name, URL, archive path or directory path, installed by `vale sync`; a style is switched on by `BasedOnStyles` (t2, Packages page). A module could ship its style directory and the consumer names its path; that is the graduation's shape, and nothing of it is shipped at founding.
- e4. **What Vale can and cannot see.** By scope it sees headings by level, list items, table cells, blockquotes, paragraphs; by `raw` it sees everything, fences included. It has no scope for emphasis, for a fence's info string or for a list's marker: those are `raw` regular expressions or a script. It reads no structure across blocks except in a script. The library's own rules show the reach: Google's `Units.yml` is an `existence` rule on `\b\d+(?:B|kB|MB|GB|TB)\b`, `Ordinal.yml` on `\d+(?:st|nd|rd|th)`, `Headings.yml` a `capitalization` rule at `scope: heading`; Microsoft's `Units.yml` flags a spelled-out number before a unit (t2, package files).
- e5. **What markdownlint covers that Vale does not.** Syntax and structure of the Markdown itself, 50-odd rules: `MD001` heading increment, `MD004` one list marker, `MD029` ordered prefixes, `MD040` a language on every fence, `MD049`/`MD050` one emphasis marker, `MD056` table column count, `MD060` table pipe style, `MD033` inline HTML, `MD013` line length, `MD025` one top-level heading, `MD059` descriptive link text (t3, rules page); custom rules in JavaScript over the parsed tree. E2 on the mixed file: 16 findings, exit 1. E2 on the kit's 110 Markdown files under the default configuration: 5,645 findings, of which 4,348 are `MD013` line length — the kit writes one paragraph per line, up to 3,791 characters — 460 `MD033` for the anchors, 441 `MD060` for the compact table pipes, 154 `MD022` for the anchor line above a heading, 83 `MD025` for a front-matter `title` beside an H1, 74 `MD029` for lists numbered from 5 or from 101 on purpose, 54 `MD031`, 5 `MD040` (the three tree fences and two more), 3 `MD024` for the two shapes in one record template, 2 `MD052` for a regular expression in prose. Five of those rules conflict with the kit's shape by design: a consumer that adopts markdownlint writes a configuration first.
- e6. **What remark-lint covers.** The same class through unified's tree, one rule per package (t4): E3 on the mixed file gives 9 warnings — list marker, ordered value, fence flag, emphasis and strong markers, heading rank — and exit 0, since warnings do not fail a run without `--frail`; no rule in the two presets counts table columns, and GitHub's alert syntax `> [!NOTE]` is reported as an undefined reference. The presets did not resolve through `npx -p`; a local install in the scratch directory did.
- e7. **Which entries and aspects each can check.** The PlantUML signatures: Vale `existence` at `raw` (E1), a markdownlint or remark custom rule, the scan of section 2. The reason line beside a signature: Vale `script` (E1), a custom rule, the scan. A block formula: the same three. ASCII art: none. Structure versus narrative: none. List marker, ordered prefixes, heading increment and depth, fence language, emphasis marker, table column count, blank lines: markdownlint and remark built in; Vale for heading depth by `heading.h4` and for the rest by `raw` regular expressions. Numbers and units: Vale, with the library's rules. Admonitions: a `raw` `existence` on `> [!`. Line length: markdownlint `MD013`, remark `maximum-line-length`.
- e8. **The boundary.** [Requirement A10.1](../requirements.md#a10-1) is Vale in CI with the consumer's ruleset; a style rule that graduates is one more YAML file in the consumer's `StylesPath`, named in `BasedOnStyles`, versioned by the release URL of e1. The module works without `const-language` ([law 3 of CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md#law-3)) and ships no Vale configuration until a default graduates.

Mechanism class: third-party linters; maturity: Vale at 3.23.0 with a package library, markdownlint at 0.41.1, remark-lint at 10.0.1; cost: none at founding.

**Recommendation: not needed at this version, CONFIRMED as not applicable until graduation; the shape recorded — the two rules of E1 as the first graduates, an `existence` rule at `scope: raw` for the signatures and a `script` rule for the reason line — and markdownlint named in the module's document as the structural linter Vale is not, for a consumer's own choice.**

## 6. The widened survey: other styling aspects at founding

The decider asked which styling aspects of document content, beyond diagrams, a style-defaults policy should consider at founding. For each: what the guides rule (t8 to t13), whether it is mechanically checkable and by what (t2 to t4), the cost, what the kit's documents do today at `0d8a48e` (E4, E5), and one verdict from the closed set — founding entry with the one-line default, lazy entry with the need that admits it, or not this module's with the home. The matrix's "exactly three founding entries" is the current ruling: an aspect recommended here as a founding entry means a matrix edit the decider makes through a decision record; this is said once and holds for every such verdict below.

- f1. **Structure versus narrative — when prose, when a list, when a table.** Google's table decides by the item: "Each item is a single unit … Use a numbered list, lettered list, or bulleted list"; "Each item is a pair of pieces of related data … Use a description list (or, in some contexts, a table)"; "Each item is three or more pieces of related data … Use a table"; and "Don't use a list to show only one item". Microsoft: "A list should have at least two items but (if possible) no more than seven items", "Don't use a table just to present a list of items that are similar. Use a list instead", a table being "two or more rows (plus a header row) and two or more columns". GitLab's translation rules ask for "Lists and tables instead of complex sentences and paragraphs". Write the Docs: "Structure content to help readers identify and skip over concepts … writing like a newspaper instead of a novel"; "Paragraphs and list items — should begin with identifiable concepts as early as possible". Diátaxis rules on the document, not the paragraph: reference "should be austere. One hardly reads reference material; one consults it", and "Adopt standard patterns"; a how-to guide is "directions that guide the reader through a problem or towards a result". Checkable: by nothing; a linter sees the form chosen, never whether the other form was due. Cost: one line and three contrastive pairs. Kit today: 921 prose lines, 1,583 dash items and 928 numbered items, 68 tables in 18 files — every table with a key column, held by rule e of `const-interaction`'s carrier — and the tables in the requirements document (1) and the module template (3) are the matrix and the template's row-shaped sections. Verdict: **founding entry** — "Prose carries reasoning and one thing at a time; a list carries items a reader will cite, each with a handle; a table only where every row has the same three or more fields and its first column is the row's key; one item is never a list, and nothing that is an argument is a table." The kit's tree already complies, the guides converge on it, and it is the aspect the decider named first — the one that costs nothing to found and everything to retrofit.
- f2. **List format — marker, numbering, punctuation, parallelism, the introducing sentence.** GitLab: "Use dashes (`-`) for unordered lists instead of asterisks (`*`). Start every item in an ordered list with `1.`"; Kubernetes: "Use the number one (`1.`) for ordered lists", any of `+`, `*`, `-` for unordered, four spaces of nesting; Google: introduce with "a complete sentence, not a partial one that's completed by the list items", a period on an item "except … If the item consists of a single word … doesn't include a verb … is entirely in code font"; Microsoft: "Make all the items in a list consistent in structure", term lists with a bold term and a period. Checkable: marker by `MD004` and remark `unordered-list-marker-style`; prefixes by `MD029` and `ordered-list-marker-value` (E2, E3); parallelism and the introducing sentence by nothing. Cost: a config line each. Kit today: dash items 1,583, star and plus 0; numbers written out and incremented (the "1/2/3" style `MD029` inferred), with 74 deliberate continuations — steps from 5 in the rollout plan, acceptance items from 101 in module documents; every item opening with a handle, green under the kit's own scan (E5). Verdict: **lazy entry** — admitted by a consumer's commit note on mixed markers or on a numbering a reader could not follow, or by the first graduation to a linter, when the default would be "dashes; numbers written out and incremented; one introducing sentence". The per-item handle is not this module's: [requirement A7.5](../requirements.md#a7-5).
- f3. **Tables — header, key column, empty cells, column count, pipe style.** Google: "introduce them with a complete sentence, use clear and concise column headings"; Microsoft: "Place information that identifies the contents of a row in the leftmost column", "Don't leave a cell blank or use an em dash … use Not applicable or None"; GitLab: "avoid empty cells … use None", a Description column rightmost, padded pipes for readability; Kubernetes: captions. Checkable: column count by `MD056` (E2: two findings on a broken table; remark's presets none); pipe style by `MD060`, which flags the kit's compact style 441 times under the default; header presence by the Markdown parser itself; the key column and "none" by nothing. Cost: config. Kit today: 68 tables, key column in every one; a module document's section that does not apply says "none" with a line of reasoning by the template's own rule. Verdict: **lazy entry** — the "when" is f1's; the "how" waits for a consumer's table a reader could not cite or a cell left blank, when the default would be "a header row, the key first, `none` in an empty cell".
- f4. **Headings and their depth.** Google: "Use sentence case", "avoid skipping heading levels or using empty headings", one h1 per page; Microsoft: "One heading level is usually plenty for a page or two", parallel structure per level; GitLab: "Do not skip a level", "If you need more than five heading levels, move the topics to a new page", H4 and below not shown in its navigation; Kubernetes: don't "Use headings level 4 through 6, unless it is absolutely necessary", sentence case, no links or bold in headings. Checkable: increment by `MD001` and remark `heading-increment` (E2, E3); depth by a Vale `existence` at `scope: heading.h4`; case by Vale `capitalization` at `scope: heading` (Google's `Headings.yml`); one H1 by `MD025`, which needs `front_matter_title` unset for envelopes with a `title`. - f5. **Code blocks and inline code.** Google: code font for "most things that have anything to do with code" — names, filenames, commands, output — and not for product names or navigational URLs; code samples wrapped at 80 characters; Microsoft: "use code style for programmatic or code-related elements", a long table of them; Kubernetes: code style for filenames, directories, paths, inline code and commands, "Use triple backticks before and after a multi-line block of code", the syntax-highlighting language optional; GitLab: "Inline code style for inputs, outputs, code", "Code blocks for command line examples". - f6. **Emphasis — bold and italic.** Google: bold "only for UI elements and run-in headings", italics "sparingly" for terms discussed and for emphasis, "your words can carry the emphasis"; Microsoft: "It's OK to use italic formatting sparingly for emphasis", bold for UI and database names, italic for new terms and mathematical variables; GitLab: bold for UI elements and navigation paths, "Do not use bold for keywords or emphasis", no italics for emphasis since "italic text does not stand out in a page using sans-serif"; Kubernetes: bold for UI elements, italics to define a new term, never bold to split paragraphs.
